3/A. Guest Mode and the Device Identifier

The App can be used without an account, in guest mode. So that a guest session is not lost every time the App is closed, the App generates a random device identifier on first launch and keeps it in the device's secure storage. It is sent with the request that creates or renews a guest session, and the server links a guest identifier (in the form G-…) to it.

  • It is stored on your device. Deleting the App, or clearing its data, removes it; the next launch generates a new one, and the previous guest session is no longer reachable.
  • No device fingerprinting. We do not derive an identifier from your device's technical characteristics (such as the user-agent, language or screen), and a guest session is not linked to your IP address or user-agent.
  • Content made in guest mode stays on the device. Places and travel plans created without an account are kept in the App, not on our servers. If you later create an account, they are transferred to it; until then, losing or replacing the device means losing them.
  • Traffic is counted, for security only. Because a large part of our map can be read without an account, we record per guest session how many requests it made in a given hour, from which network (the address truncated to its network prefix — IPv4 /24, IPv6 /48 — never the full IP address), and with which platform and app version. This is used to detect and stop automated bulk extraction and abuse, on the basis of our legitimate interest. It is not used for analytics, profiling or advertising.
  • Pseudonymous, not anonymous. These identifiers do not contain your name or email address, but under applicable data protection law they are still personal data, and this Policy treats them as such.

The guest token issued to the device is valid for 30 days and is renewed automatically by the App while it is in use, with the same device identifier.

3/B. Reports and Contributions to OpenStreetMap

Much of our map comes from OpenStreetMap (OSM), a public map database. When you report that a place has changed or propose a new place, the report is checked automatically and then reviewed by a person, who may correct it or reject it. Accepted place data is then either written to OSM or left for local OSM mappers as a public note, under our own OSM account and never in your name. You can withdraw a report in the App until it has been reviewed.

  • What goes to OSM: only place data — for example name, type, opening hours, fees, accessibility, and a place's description as approved by the reviewer. For a report made near the place, the day of the report is recorded as the day the place was checked on site.
  • What never goes to OSM: your name, account, email address, photos, Wi-Fi passwords, the note you wrote to us, or your location.
  • What we keep: the report itself, its review and its outcome, linked to your account while the account exists. OSM data is published under the Open Database License and cannot be withdrawn from OSM once published; see section 4.4 of the Terms of Service.

Please do not put personal data (your own or anyone else's) into a place's name or description.

4. Email Communication

We send service emails needed to operate the App, such as password reset, security notices, trip invitations you asked us to send, and information about changes affecting your use of the App.

We send the newsletter only if you asked for it: the box is unticked when you register, and you can change your choice at any time in the App. Every newsletter contains a link that unsubscribes you in one step, without signing in. We record when you subscribed and unsubscribed, to be able to prove your consent.

If a mail server permanently rejects an address, or a recipient marks our email as spam, we put that address on a do-not-email list so that we never write to it again. This list holds only the address, the reason and the date; it is kept even after the account is deleted, because its only purpose is to make sure that address is not written to again.

5. Analytics, Crash Reporting and Product Tools in the App

The App uses PostHog (product analytics, feature flags, surveys, and limited session replay where enabled) and Google Firebase (Analytics, Crashlytics crash reports, Performance Monitoring and In-App Messaging) to understand how the App is used and to fix errors.

These tools run only with your consent. Until you grant it, they collect and send nothing — the App works exactly the same either way. You can withdraw consent at any time in the App’s settings, which stops any further collection.

PostHog data is processed on PostHog’s EU infrastructure. Where session replay is enabled, masking is applied to reduce the likelihood of capturing text entered in the App or other sensitive content. Firebase is provided by Google Ireland Limited; see section 9 for transfers outside the EEA.

If you allow notifications, the App receives a push token from Firebase Cloud Messaging so that we can send you notifications. You can turn notifications off in your device settings at any time.

6/A. Website Cookies and Consent (Google Analytics 4)

The Voyager Map website uses its own consent banner to collect, store, and manage cookie preferences. Until a choice is made, Google Consent Mode is set to denied for analytics, advertising, and personalisation, so nothing non-essential runs before consent is given.

  • Strictly necessary cookies: used for essential website operation and security.
  • Statistics cookies: include Google Analytics 4 (GA4) for aggregate usage analytics.
  • Marketing cookies: used only if enabled and only with consent where required.

On the website, GA4 is configured to load only after the user grants consent for statistics cookies. If consent is refused, GA4 does not load and no GA4 analytics events are sent.

Legal basis for non-essential website cookies (including GA4): consent. Users can withdraw or change consent at any time via the cookie settings interface.

Where Google services involve transfers outside the EEA, such transfers are handled under applicable safeguards (for example, Standard Contractual Clauses, where applicable).

6/B. Cookie Retention (Website)

Cookie lifetimes vary by cookie type and provider. Strictly necessary cookies, including the record of your own consent choice, are kept for up to 12 months. Google Analytics 4 cookies are set only after consent and follow Google’s own lifetimes for that product. You can review or change your choice at any time through the consent banner, and clearing your browser storage removes the stored choice.

7. Server Logs and Diagnostics

Our servers record technical data such as request time, IP address, user-agent and error information, for operation and security (legitimate interest). The ordinary request log is rotated and kept only briefly. Warnings and errors, which may include the IP address and user-agent of the request that caused them, are kept for 30 days so that problems can be investigated. Refused requests (for example, too many sign-in attempts) are counted per minute, with the IP address, and kept for 30 days.

10. Data Retention

Data is retained only as long as necessary for the purpose it was collected for. In particular:

  • Account, profile, preferences, saved places and trips: until you delete the account.
  • Guest sessions: deleted after 90 days without a visit. A session is only ever restored for a returning guest within 30 days.
  • Hourly guest request counts: deleted after 30 days.
  • Content created in guest mode: held on your device, for as long as you keep the App installed; deleting the App deletes it.
  • Server warnings and errors, refused requests: 30 days (section 7).
  • Consent records: kept after account deletion, without any other data about you, for as long as we may need to prove that consent was given (in general, the 5-year limitation period).
  • Do-not-email list: for as long as we send email (section 4).
  • Reports and proposed changes: for as long as the map data they concern is kept; after account deletion without any link to you.

10/A. What happens when you delete your account

Your account, profile, sign-in methods, consents, favourites, private places and the trips only you are part of are deleted. Content that other people rely on is kept, but no longer linked to you:

  • public places, and places someone else has saved or put in their trip;
  • images still shown with such content;
  • trips shared with others, which pass to another member;
  • your reports and proposed changes, and place data already published on OpenStreetMap.

If such remaining content itself identifies you (for example, a photo or a description), you can ask us to remove it at the address in section 1. After the deletion we keep only an anonymous count of deleted accounts per month, App language and sign-in method; it contains nothing that could identify you. See the Delete Your Account and Data page for how to delete your account.
